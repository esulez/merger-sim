# Merger simulation: core literature

## Structural / foundational

**Berry, Levinsohn & Pakes (1995), "Automobile Prices in Market Equilibrium", Econometrica 63(4), 841-890.**
The random-coefficients BLP demand model. Introduces the contraction mapping that separates non-linear
(heterogeneity) parameters from linear (mean utility) ones, making rich demand estimation tractable. The
flexible demand side that serious merger simulations build on; the source of the supply-side logic
(differentiated Bertrand + ownership matrices) used everywhere below.

**Werden & Froeb (1994), "The Effects of Mergers in Differentiated Products Industries: Logit Demand and
Merger Policy", Journal of Law, Economics & Organization 10(2), 407-426.**
The canonical simple merger simulation: logit demand, Bertrand pricing, equilibrium recomputation before
and after an ownership change. Establishes the minimal calibration logic (shares + a price coefficient)
and shows how logit diversion ratios drive predicted price effects.

**Hausman, Leonard & Zona (1994), "Competitive Analysis with Differentiated Products", Annales
d'Economie et de Statistique 34, 159-180.**
Multi-market (nested) demand estimation applied to beer; an early template for empirical merger
simulation in differentiated products industries.

## Diagnostics and screening

**Werden (1996), "A Robust Test for Consumer Welfare Enhancing Mergers Among Sellers of Differentiated
Products", Journal of Industrial Economics 44(4), 409-413.**
Derives the upward pricing pressure condition: the smallest margin for which a merger cannot raise
consumer prices. Precursor of modern UPP/GUPPI screening.

**Epstein & Rubinfeld (2001), "Merger Simulation: A Simplified Approach with New Technology", Antitrust
Law Journal 69(3).**
PCAIDS: proportional-calibration with only a few shares and one elasticity. The low-data-cost alternative
when full demand estimation is infeasible.

## Post-merger conduct and identification

**Nevo (2000), "Mergers with Differentiated Products: The Case of the Ready-to-Eat Cereal Industry",
RAND Journal of Economics 31(2), 395-421.**
Full structural application to the cereal industry (the classic teaching dataset). Compares conduct
assumptions and stresses sensitivity of predicted effects to how post-merger efficiency gains are
modelled.

**Farrell & Shapiro (2010), "Antitrust Evaluation of Horizontal Mergers: An Economic Alternative to
Market Definition", B.E. Journal of Theoretical Economics 10(1).**
Formalises the diversion-ratio/margin screen (UPP) that became GUPPI in the 2010 US Horizontal Merger
Guidelines; the intellectual basis for screening without market definition.

**Miller & Weinberg (2017), "Understanding the Price Effects of the MillerCoors/JV", RAND (and Miller,
Remer & Sheu (2013) "Pass-Through and the Prediction of Merger Price Effects".** Pass-through adjustments
to UPP; second-order refinement when margins/calibration are rough.

## Enforcement guidance using these models

- US DOJ/FTC **Horizontal Merger Guidelines (2010, rev. 2023)**, Sections 6-7: diversion ratios and
  upward pricing pressure are now standard screening tools.
- UK CMA **Merger Assessment Guidelines**, and EC **Best Practices**: both reference quantitative
  simulation as supporting evidence.
